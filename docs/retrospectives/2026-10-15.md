## The central finding: the review loop's baseline

**The review loop's cost is an application failure, not a missing-rule failure.** Over the window, 87 rounds across 10
units carried 145 compliance-miss findings — findings the record itself flags as a miss of a rule the repository already
carries — and 79% of rounds carried at least one. Three captures in the window returned "nothing durable" for exactly
that reason: the loop's most expensive unit is a product one at 19 rounds, with its own meta-units close behind at 16.
The one review-loop change the record did support was the canonical class list, because it fixed a _signal_ rather than
adding prose.

**Baseline (quote verbatim):** the window-proper figures **excluding this retrospective's own rows** — its record rows
grow as the review loop runs, so the deriving command filters them (`$2 != "retrospective-2026-10-15"`) and a later
window must apply the same exclusion: _for the window opening 2026-10-14 — `docs/review-rounds.tsv` rows dated

**The boundary that makes it stable:** the digest's aggregation filter is `date >= <since>`, so its `window:` line
printed **130 rounds / 203 misses** for `since=2026-10-13` when this retrospective measured it — inclusive of the
previous window's tail. The window-proper figure is that filter minus the rows dated on the `since` date, so a future
window must apply the same one-day subtraction, or quote the digest's line with its `since` stated.

**Label normalization (the canonical list landed 2026-10-15, #577).** The window's rounds were recorded pre-vocabulary:
**46 distinct class labels over 87 rounds**. Recomputed through the mapping #577's design records (46 → **18** canonical
classes), the share of rounds raising a class a prior round had raised — recomputed from the raised labels rather than
the record's `repeated` column, which is why it differs from the 68% above — moves **70% → 74%** — the drifting labels
_understated_ the loop's non-convergence. One label, `claims-table-anchor`, appears in exactly one row whose `repeated`
column names it — a label no prior round had raised.

**Do the 2026-10-13 levers show any effect? Not yet, and this window cannot answer it.**

- The visibility lever landed: every window unit that has a change dir carries the pre-write pass ticked in its
  `tasks.md` — eight of the ten — and #567's claims-table mechanism is dogfooded by each of them.
- No reduction is visible: 87 rounds over 10 units (mean 8.7, median 8), with the review loop's own meta-units among the
  costliest at 16 rounds, behind a product unit at 19; the single-round unit is a docs edit.
- Why the window cannot answer: the units ran **concurrent with the levers' landing**; the **canonical list landed at
  the very end**, so almost every round carries pre-canonical labels (all but the canonical list's own) and the repeat
  share is computed from labels that do not compare; the review loop's own meta-units are **self-referential** (they
  re-derive the pass's own rules while the review applies them); and the record's `compliance_misses` column is **per
  round, not per class**, so it cannot attribute a miss to a class.

**What the rounds are evidence for:** the per-unit round cost, the share of rounds re-raising a class, the vocabulary's
drift before the canonical list, and the pre-write pass being visible and ticked. **What they are not evidence for:**
whether the pass or the canonical list _reduces_ rounds — the first window whose rounds are both post-lever and
canonically labelled is the next one.

## Lessons carried through the window

Recorded in `AGENTS.md` during the window, by grepping each `captured:` token rather than listing from memory: the
**claims table as the pass's output** and the **prior-instance** pass, with the fourth root-cause shape (a claims
table's completeness — delete the unneeded fact rather than add the row) and the **delete-and-shrink** escalation
(#567); a **defect the applying command rewrites is not a finding** (#569); a **check's control is a committed test**,
with the `*RulesTests` shape (#568); the **measurement-attribution** rule — window-scope a retained series from counter
or histogram deltas over the measurement's own window, never a `*_max` gauge (#570); the **p99 confirm-first** rule and
the **Spotless-skip** clause (a skipped file is indistinguishable from a formatted one, #574, #575); and the
**retrospective-suggestion sweep** (#566). The tail added the pre-write pass's record, the per-round record line, the
class-exhaustion clause, and the digest's derived facts. Two of the window's captures are notable for what they are not:
`dont-report-what-a-gate-covers` and `require-check-controls-as-tests` both **route a recurring class to a mechanism**
rather than adding prose.

## Went well

- **The canonical class list was the one review-loop change with evidence of a real defect, and it is a mechanism, not
  prose** — the record held 55 labels before the canonical list's rounds were recorded, 56 after them — for a handful of
  phenomena, and one used once while recorded as _repeated_.
- **The Prettier misalignment was a genuine silent defect**, and the window both removed its trigger (#575) and added a
  mechanical guard against recurrence (#576).
- **Two review classes became mechanisms** (#568, #569) instead of more `AGENTS.md` prose — the direction the previous
  retrospective asked for.
- **`AGENTS.md` growth slowed sharply:** 2,965 → **3,033 lines (+68)** against +272 the previous window; top-level
  bullets **172 → 172 (net 0)**; `captured:` lines 162 → 168.
- **Product work was verified by measurement**, not by a build gate: the tail attributed by a layered run (#570), the
  drift check's verdict refined from it (#574).

## Went wrong

- **The review loop's cost did not fall.** Its most expensive unit is a product one (19 rounds), and the review loop's
  own meta-units follow close behind (16) — a cost the loop's own captures could not reduce.
- **`give-the-review-a-canonical-class-list`'s capture returned "nothing durable"** — every class it found was a
  compliance miss of a rule the file already carries, further evidence that the cost is an _application_ failure rather
  than a missing-rule failure.
- **The class vocabulary had drifted for the whole window** (46 labels over 87 rounds), so the loop's convergence signal
  was unreliable until the last PR.
- **A window unit archived with unticked verification tasks.** `derive-the-change-claims` archived with its
  `review-quick` pass and manual-pass request boxes unchecked (verified in the archived file). The 16 recorded rounds
  show the review ran, so this may be bookkeeping rather than a skipped pass — either way, the record cannot tell.

**S1 — make the digest's aggregation comparable and quotable.** **Parked** in `docs/ideas.md` (2026-10-17). Normalize
rows recorded before the canonical list through #577's mapping before computing the classes-raised and repeat shares,
and print the window-proper figures beside the inclusive count. The design already says a later digest may normalize by
the mapping, and the derived-facts block's review-rounds clause is specified in `agent-skills`, so this is a `MODIFIED`
delta rather than a new rule.

**S2 — check that every record label is in the reviewer's canonical list.** **Parked** in `docs/ideas.md` (2026-10-17).
A rules-object check with its known-bad and known-good cases, asserting that every class in the record recorded on or
after the canonical boundary is in the reviewer's list, failing when one is not (so a new class is added to the contract
first — exactly #577's extension rule). The vocabulary is stated as prose today, and the loop's whole convergence signal
depends on label comparability, which this window showed drifting.

**S3 — park the rejected review-loop levers durably (applied).** A proposal the owner brought to this window — to
optimize the iterative review loop — named four levers the record contradicts: a persistent reviewer session,
diff-scoped follow-ups, a fixed iteration cap, and token/time telemetry. The repository's rule that work a change
surfaces is parked durably already requires a home for that verdict, so it is parked in `docs/ideas.md` rather than
re-derived the next time the question is asked.

**S4 — guard against unticked tasks in an archived change dir.** **Applied** in `guard-archived-task-boxes`: the
pre-commit guard refuses a staged `openspec/changes/archive/**/tasks.md` carrying an unticked task, judged over the
whole staged blob, with no task exemption (a deferred task is reworded and ticked) and an incoming-merge exemption by
blob equality. Originally parked in `docs/ideas.md` (2026-10-17). Eight archived change dirs carry an unticked
manual-review-pass box (nine carry some unticked review-gate box), two of them window-proper
(`derive-the-change-claims`, `require-check-controls-as-tests`) — a documented hazard that no gate enforces; the fix is
the mechanism (a `check` member or a pre-commit arm refusing to stage an archived change dir with unticked boxes,
exempting an owner-decoupled task). Lower confidence: the exemption logic is fiddly, and the instance may be bookkeeping
— park it if the guard reads as over-engineering.

**Not proposed:** the self-referential cost of the review loop's own meta-units; attributing compliance misses to
classes (the per-round column already answers this window's question); and any new `AGENTS.md` rule — every window
capture is already in the file.

## Recommended next direction

**Measure the next window before changing the loop again.** The baseline above is the number any further review-loop
change should be judged against, and the window it comes from cannot yet show whether the levers work — so the next
direction is the comparison, not another lever. Parked in `docs/ideas.md`, S1 and S2 are the two that make that
comparison possible (a comparable aggregation and a label check); S4 is a mechanism for a documented hazard, worth
taking if its exemption logic is judged cheap. The product direction the earlier retrospectives asked for is still the
one this window served best: `attribute-load-test-tail` and `make-the-p99-verdict-confirm-first` shipped real, measured
behavior.
